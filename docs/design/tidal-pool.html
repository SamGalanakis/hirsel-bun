Tidal Pool

Specs are rock pools on a shore. Each pool is a contained micro-ecosystem. Tasks are the creatures living inside — anemones, crabs, starfish. Workers are waves washing over: each wave carries new work in and completed work out. High tide is dispatch. Low tide is review.

Why this is interesting

Waves = worker dispatch

Workers are not persistent residents — they are waves. Each wave washes over a pool, deposits new work, and recedes. High tide means the dispatcher has filled every pool with activity. You feel the rhythm of the system in the rhythm of the waves.

Creatures = living tasks

Tasks are not checkboxes — they are organisms. An anemone gently pulsing, a crab sidling, a starfish slowly turning. Each has its own lifecycle. A thriving pool full of diverse creatures means a well-decomposed spec with all its tasks alive and working.

Tide cycles = review cadence

Low tide is when you can see everything clearly. The water recedes, and every creature in every pool is visible and inspectable. This maps to review phases — when activity pauses, you can see the state of every spec at a glance.

Empty pools = potential

A dry pool on the rocks is not dead — it is waiting. When the tide comes in, it will fill with water and life will colonize it. Draft specs are empty pools: the infrastructure is there, the niche exists, but no wave has reached it yet.
