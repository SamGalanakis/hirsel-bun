Mycelium Net

An underground fungal network. The route is the forest floor. Specs are fruiting bodies — mushrooms — visible on the surface. Below ground, tasks are hyphal threads connecting specs. Workers are nutrient pulses flowing through the hyphae, visible as glowing dots tracing connections.

Mushroom = Spec

Visible on the surface. Full cap means validated. Emerging cap is working. A spore site is a draft not yet germinated.

Hyphae = Tasks

Underground threads connecting specs. Active hyphae glow brighter. The network reveals how specs share dependencies beneath the surface.

Nutrient Pulse = Worker

Glowing dots flowing through hyphae. Each pulse is a worker carrying intent from decomposition to execution along the fungal network.

Why this is interesting

Hidden complexity, visible results

Users see mushrooms on the surface — simple status indicators. But the real orchestration happens underground in the hyphal network. This mirrors how Hirsel works: specs are the visible intent, but the task decomposition and worker coordination is the invisible engine beneath.

Networks, not hierarchies

Mycelium connects everything to everything. A task from one spec can share nutrients with another spec's tasks. This models the reality that specs often share dependencies and workers can cross-pollinate — it's a network, not a tree.

Growth is organic

Mushrooms don't appear instantly — they emerge. The working state shows a cap pushing through the soil. Blocked specs are buried. Validated specs bloom fully. The lifecycle of a spec becomes a visible, tangible growth process on the forest floor.

Delivery is sporulation

A mature mushroom releases spores — sending its validated work out into the world. Delivery in Hirsel packages completed specs and pushes them as PRs. The spores land in the repository, seeding the next generation of the codebase.
