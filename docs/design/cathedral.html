Why this is interesting

Bottom-up dependency

You cannot build the spire before the foundation. The cathedral enforces build order visually — the structure literally cannot stand without the lower sections. This maps exactly to spec dependencies in Hirsel. Blocked specs are unbuilt sections above.

Scaffolding shows work in progress

Active construction is unmistakable: scaffolding, cranes, and partial walls. You see exactly where the mason crews are working and how much stone is laid. When the section is done, the scaffolding comes down to reveal solid stone. The before/after is dramatic.

Blueprints are drafts

Unstarted specs appear as architectural drawings pinned to the wall — plans that exist but haven't been realized yet. You can see the intent (rose window design) without it being built. Moving from blueprint to stone is the draft-to-working transition.

The building tells time

A cathedral under construction encodes its entire history. The foundation is ancient and weathered. The walls are solid and established. The transept has fresh mortar. The spire is just an aspiration. Every layer of the building is a layer of project progress.
